# Lane simulation and TI1 hero pool

The match engine is a TypeScript adaptation of the user's **Lane Siege** HTML prototype, preserved in `reference/lane-siege-original.html`. The original repository entry point and README are preserved separately as `reference/original-index.html` and `reference/original-README.md`; the original commit history is retained in the publishing checkout. Reused concepts: three lanes, two towers per lane, creep waves, attribute scaling, basic attacks, four ability archetypes, healing and retreat, nearby experience, respawn, and Ancient destruction.

The TI1 pool contains 46 heroes documented as contested at The International 2011 by [Spectral Stats](https://stats.ru.spectral.gg/lrg2/?league=ti1&mod=heroes), cross-checked against [Liquipedia's TI1 hero statistics](https://liquipedia.net/dota2/The_International/Hero_Statistics/2011). This includes Axe, who was banned but not picked. Modern names are used for renamed heroes. Hero membership is sourced; all combat values, role preferences, and ability archetypes are deliberately simplified placeholders. Later starting years currently retain this classic pool, pending additional era data.

Results are best-of-three event deciders, not full tournament brackets. Actual Ancient destructions decide each game; two game wins decide the series. Gold and XP series are sampled from the engine's running totals. Gold represents accumulated resources with abstract automatic item scaling, not a complete shop/inventory economy. Assists count distinct recent enemy-hero damage contributors, excluding the credited killer. Creeps can secure uncredited hero kills. Nearby allies share XP; levels cap at 25. A hero's cumulative XP continues to be recorded at the cap.

The simulation runs in a Web Worker. Watching, speeding up, pausing, and skipping affect only playback, never the outcome or rewards. Frames stay in memory; small scoreboards and resource timelines persist with campaign history. Old saves/results remain readable; old roll-based results do not invent detailed match data.

One engine second represents six match-clock seconds. Combat constants are calibrated for a bounded prototype. Late-game creep siege ramps up to break stalemates. After the overtime threshold, both Ancients take periodic siege attrition weighted by the opposing side's surviving heroes and destroyed towers. Overtime is identified in the result. In the rare case that both Ancients fall on the same tick, accumulated team gold breaks the tie. There is no win-probability coin flip or predetermined winner.
